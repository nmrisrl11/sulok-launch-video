## 1. Project Overview

Create a short, polished motion-introductory video for **Sulok**. This
should feel like a brand story with product demonstrations---not a
conventional feature walkthrough or a slideshow.

The video should help viewers recognize a familiar problem, understand
why Sulok was built, see how it helps, and remember the product.

- **Target duration:** 45--60 seconds; aim for approximately 55
  seconds.
- **Primary format:** 16:9, 1920 × 1080.
- **Frame rate:** 30 fps.
- **Primary delivery:** MP4/H.264, with a high-quality render suitable
  for a website launch and social posting.
- **Tone:** Personal, relatable, thoughtful, modern, warm, quietly
  playful, and polished.
- **Story flow:** Opening problem → personal motivation and reveal →
  product experience → closing brand message.
- **Creative contrast:** Begin with visual clutter and mild
  frustration; transition into a calm, clear, inviting experience.

Do not make the video feel aggressive, overly corporate, or like a
generic SaaS advertisement. Keep the story human.

## 3. Product Story and Motivation

The video is rooted in a personal experience:

- Links and bookmarks ended up in different browsers.
- Bookmark folders became nested, cluttered, disorganized, or
  outdated.
- Some saved links were hard to find again.
- Duplicate bookmarks sometimes accumulated.
- Open tabs on a phone became a reminder to revisit something
  later---but were often forgotten.
- Sometimes links were sent to a personal chat as another temporary
  storage place.
- Browser bookmark syncing can depend on signing in, which the creator
  did not consistently use.
- The underlying problem was not a lack of places to save links; it
  was the difficulty of keeping them accessible and rediscovering
  them.

Sulok was created as a personal corner of the web: a place to save,
organize, and rediscover things found online.

### Core message

**The internet is full of things worth keeping. Give them a place of
their own.**

### Recommended narrative

1.  **Recognition:** "I've done that." Show familiar saving habits and
    digital clutter.
2.  **Motivation:** "That's why I built Sulok." Make the personal reason
    clear.
3.  **Understanding:** "I can save, organize, and find things again."
    Demonstrate the product.
4.  **Recall:** Leave viewers with the brand, tagline, and app URL.

Do not portray browser bookmarks as universally bad or broken. The
problem is the creator's fragmented personal workflow and the difficulty
of finding saved links again.

## 4. Creative Direction

### Opening: digital clutter

The opening should communicate the problem visually before explaining
it. Possible visual motifs:

- Browser tabs multiplying.
- Nested bookmark folders being opened one after another.
- A long list of saved links that is difficult to scan.
- A phone with many open tabs.
- A link being sent to a personal chat.
- A repeated or duplicated saved link.
- A cursor searching, scrolling, and opening folders without finding
  the desired item.

Use purposeful pacing, close crops, overlapping windows, and subtle
motion to build a feeling of clutter. Do not create a visually
incomprehensible mess that viewers cannot recognize.

### Reveal: clutter becomes a corner

At the transition, stop the frantic movement and create a short moment
of silence or reduced sound. Let the scattered items settle, fade, or
converge into one coherent space. Introduce the Sulok logo and a
restrained Sulo mascot moment.

Preferred reveal line:

**"That's why I built Sulok."**

This line connects the product to the creator's personal story more
naturally than a generic "Introducing Sulok" announcement.

### Product section: real product, clear interactions

Use the real Sulok app, real UI components, and available feature-guide
assets whenever practical. The product should remain recognizable and
credible.

Prioritize these three demonstrations:

1.  **Save:** Enter or paste a URL and show the save flow or preview.
2.  **Organize:** Show saved links as a clear, readable collection; use
    folders only if the real UI and feature are available in the current
    build.
3.  **Rediscover:** Search for a saved link and open the result.

Keep each interaction brief, readable, and intentional. Do not try to
demonstrate every feature in the launch video.

### Closing: calm, personal, memorable

Return to the warm brand palette and a clean composition. Show the Sulok
logo, tagline, and production URL. A small Sulo blink or expression
change may add personality, but the logo and call to action must remain
the focus.

Suggested end card:

**Sulok**\
Your corner of the web.\
**Save. Organize. Rediscover.**\
https://sulok-app.vercel.app/

## 5. Scene-by-Scene Storyboard

Target runtime: approximately 55 seconds at 30 fps (about 1,650 frames).
Treat these timings as an initial edit, not immutable rules. The final
composition must not exceed the agreed target duration without a reason.

### Scene 1 --- The problem (0:00--0:12)

**Purpose:** Make viewers recognize their own habits.

**Visuals:** - Start with a browser window or tabs in a restrained
composition. - Add tabs, folders, or saved links in a deliberate
sequence. - Cut to a phone with open tabs or a personal message
containing a link. - Show the user opening nested folders or scrolling
through saved items. - End with the search attempt unresolved.

**On-screen copy, shown one at a time:** - "I'll check it later." -
"Where did I save that?" - "Wait... did I already save this?"

**Motion:** Gradually increase pace and visual density. Avoid constant
motion in every element; preserve a clear focal point.

**Sound:** Start with subtle keyboard and click sounds. Build a
restrained rhythmic layer as the clutter grows. Stop or sharply reduce
the sound at the transition.

**Transition:** Freeze or slow the scene, then fade or move the clutter
away.

### Scene 2 --- The reveal (0:12--0:20)

**Purpose:** Connect the problem to the product's personal motivation.

**Visuals:** - Let the clutter stop. - Bring a few scattered links
toward a single organized space. - Reveal the actual Sulok identity and,
optionally, Sulo. - Transition into the real app interface.

**Voice-over option:** "I found myself saving things everywhere... and
forgetting them just as easily. So I built something of my own."

**On-screen headline:** "That's why I built Sulok."

**Supporting line:** "Your corner of the web."

**Motion:** Smooth, deliberate, and noticeably calmer than the opening.

### Scene 3 --- Save (0:20--0:27)

**Purpose:** Demonstrate how a link enters Sulok.

**Visuals:** - Show a URL being entered or pasted into the actual app
flow. - Show the real preview/save interaction if available in the
current app. - Finish with the saved item appearing in the collection.

**Voice-over option:** "Save the things that catch your attention."

**On-screen keyword:** "Save."

**Implementation note:** Do not fake a working feature if the current
application does not support the depicted flow. If the real flow
differs, adapt the storyboard to the actual UI.

### Scene 4 --- Organize (0:27--0:35)

**Purpose:** Communicate that saved links can live in a more useful,
recognizable space.

**Visuals:** - Show real saved-item cards with available titles,
favicons, previews, or descriptions. - If folders are part of the
current implementation, show a concise drag-and-drop or folder
interaction. - Keep the result readable and avoid filling the entire
frame with tiny UI.

**Voice-over option:** "Keep everything together, organized your way."

**On-screen keyword:** "Organize."

### Scene 5 --- Rediscover (0:35--0:43)

**Purpose:** Show the practical payoff: finding a saved link again.

**Visuals:** - Use the actual search or command-palette interaction
available in the current build. - Enter a short query. - Let the results
resolve to one clearly identifiable link. - Open it or highlight it as
the successful result.

**Voice-over option:** "And find your way back whenever you need to."

**On-screen keyword:** "Rediscover."

**Implementation note:** Use a feature that exists in the current
application. Do not imply that every feature is available on every
platform unless verified.

### Scene 6 --- Closing (0:43--0:55)

**Purpose:** Leave the viewer with the product name, meaning, and next
step.

**Visuals:** - Let the interface settle and transition to a warm, clean
end card. - Show the Sulok logo or approved brand mark. - Optionally
animate Sulo with a subtle blink or suitable expression. - Reveal the
tagline, three-word product promise, and URL. - Hold the end card long
enough to read comfortably.

**Voice-over option:** "Because the things you find online deserve a
place to call home. Sulok. Your corner of the web."

**End card copy:**

Sulok\
Your corner of the web.\
Save. Organize. Rediscover.\
https://sulok-app.vercel.app/

**Sound:** Resolve the music gently. Keep the final moment uncluttered.

## 7. Motion, Typography, and Sound

### Motion principles

- Use smooth easing and clear motion hierarchy.
- Reserve faster cuts and overlapping movement for the problem
  opening.
- Use clean transitions for the reveal and product scenes.
- Avoid excessive zooms, spins, bounce effects, and gratuitous
  parallax.
- Keep key text and UI comfortably inside safe margins.
- Ensure every motion supports the story or directs attention.

### Typography

- Inspect the existing project and brand assets for the actual fonts
  and typography conventions.
- If the font cannot be verified, choose a restrained, highly readable
  sans-serif and keep it consistent.
- Avoid tiny captions and overly long text overlays.
- Use the supplied palette for text and accents.

### Sound design

- Use licensed, original, or otherwise properly usable music and sound
  effects.
- Do not assume an audio file exists. Inspect the assets first.
- If no suitable audio is available, make the composition work
  silently and document where music/SFX can be added.
- Keep sound effects subordinate to narration and readable on-screen
  text.
- If narration is unavailable, the story must still make sense without
  it.

## 8. Remotion Production Requirements

- Use Remotion for the video composition, animation, timing, and
  rendering.
- Use a clear root composition and small, reusable scene components.
- Define video dimensions, fps, and duration centrally.
- Prefer frame-based animation using Remotion's timing and
  interpolation APIs.
- Avoid direct dependence on browser timers, random values that change
  across renders, or non-deterministic animation.
- Use deterministic data and stable asset paths so repeated renders
  are consistent.
- Keep scene boundaries and transition durations easy to adjust.
- Use the existing project conventions where possible.
- Do not rewrite the Sulok application to create the video.
- Do not copy the whole app into the video project without a reason.
  Reuse assets and UI selectively.
- If importing actual application components is impractical, create a
  faithful, clearly identified mockup based on the real current UI and
  explain that choice.
- Avoid adding a dependency if Remotion or the existing stack already
  provides the required capability.
- Keep TypeScript strict; **never use TypeScript `any`**.
- Keep the implementation accessible where applicable, maintainable,
  and easy to iterate on.
- Make the main text, URL, scene durations, and key colors easy to
  update.

## 9. Asset and Feature Verification

Before building: 1. Inspect the current working directory and repository
structure. 2. Read `public/app-info.ts` for the product's current
descriptions and verified claims. 3. Inspect `public/mascots`,
`public/folder-icon`, and `public/feature-guides`. 4. Inspect
`src/pages/guide` and the deployed `/guide` page where access is

current build. 6. Identify available fonts, logos, audio, screenshots,
and relevant UI components. 7. Record any missing assets or conflicts
with this brief.

Treat the actual codebase and assets as the source of truth for product
behavior. This brief provides the intended story; it does not prove that
every feature described in older notes is currently implemented.

## 10. Content and Claim Guardrails

- Do not claim that Sulok is cloud-synced, account-free in every
  situation, encrypted, or universally private unless the current
  implementation and approved product copy support the exact claim.
- The product is described as local-first. Verify the current behavior
  before making a privacy or storage claim.
- P2P device sync, browser extension behavior, bookmark import, file
  import, recycle bin, notes, favorites, and command palette should be
  shown only if verified and only if they help the short narrative.
- Avoid invented UI controls, fabricated feature interactions,
  incorrect folder behavior, or inaccurate mascot assets.
- Do not imply that a feature exists on mobile, desktop, or in the
  browser extension unless verified.
- Preserve the personal origin story without suggesting every user has
  the same problem.

## 11. Accessibility and Delivery Quality

- Ensure on-screen text has strong contrast and enough time to be
  read.
- Keep essential text away from the edges.
- Do not rely on color alone to communicate meaning.
- Make the video understandable without sound through on-screen text
  and visual storytelling.
- If narration is used, provide a script and make sure captions match
  it.
- Check for text clipping, flicker, awkward easing, visible asset
  loading, broken image paths, and inconsistent timing.
- Review the entire rendered video at full speed, not only individual
  frames.
- Render a final MP4 and keep the project able to reproduce it.

## 12. Definition of Done

The work is complete when: - The video tells a clear problem → reveal →
product → closing story. - The output is approximately 45--60 seconds
and 1920 × 1080 at 30 fps. - The actual Sulok identity and suitable
approved assets are used. - The product scenes match verified
functionality. - All scene timings and transitions are deterministic. -
The composition renders successfully without missing assets or runtime
errors. - The final video has been inspected for pacing, legibility, and
visual defects. - The final render path, render command, and any known
limitations are documented. - No changes have been made to the Sulok
production app unless separately requested.
